Adsorbents ​

Mocca's library holds the adsorbents of published studies. Load one by name with load_adsorbent, and list them with library_adsorbents():

julia
using Mocca
library_adsorbents()
adsorbent = load_adsorbent("zeolite_13x_haghpanah_2013")

Each has a dual-site Langmuir isotherm (see Model equations) and the particle properties that set the mass transfer rate. The figures show the pure-component isotherms in mol/kg at three temperatures, computed with isotherm_table; the tables give the parameters as they are in the file, with capacities in mol per m³ of adsorbent particles.

Zeolite 13X (Haghpanah et al. 2013) ​

The adsorbent of the four-step and two-bed VSA processes, for dry flue gas. Library name "zeolite_13x_haghpanah_2013".

Zeolite 13X (Krishnamurthy et al. 2014) ​

The adsorbent of the wet flue gas processes. Its CO₂ and N₂ parameters are close to those above, and it adds water. Water is held very strongly: at 25 °C the zeolite is nearly saturated with it below 0.5 kPa. Library name "zeolite_13x_krishnamurthy_2014".

Silica gel (Krishnamurthy et al. 2014) ​

The water-removal bed of the dual-adsorbent process. It holds about a fifth as much CO₂ as zeolite 13X at 1 bar, and takes up water more gradually. Library name "silica_gel_krishnamurthy_2014".

Your own adsorbent ​

Write the isotherm with dual_site_langmuir or langmuir, make an Adsorbent and save it with save_adsorbent to use it again; the Building a process from its parts example does this, and Process files shows the file format. Check it against the source with equilibrium_loading and isotherm_table, which report mol/kg. Other isotherm models can be added with register_isotherm!.
