Gas properties ​

The gas of a bed is an ideal gas: its density and concentration follow in each cell. Its heat capacity, viscosity and diffusivities come from two models held by the Gas, and both are constant by default:

PropertyDefaultChanging with the state of each cell
Heat capacityConstant per component, as given (heat_capacities)thermodynamics = :polynomial: cubic in temperature; :shomate: the Shomate equation
ViscosityThe value at the feed (viscosity)transport = :chapman_enskog: kinetic theory with Wilke's mixing rule
Molecular diffusivitiesThe values at the feed (diffusivity)transport = :chapman_enskog: kinetic theory with Wilke's mixture rule
Axial dispersionFixed,  With a transport model: Wakao and Funazkri, per component and face
Axial thermal conductivityThe Column's axial_conductivityAlways constant

The equations are in Model equations: see Energy balance of the column, Axial dispersion and Transport properties.

Choosing the models ​

julia
using Mocca
gas = Gas(["CO2", "N2", "H2O"]; thermodynamics = :polynomial, transport = :chapman_enskog)

The two choices are independent. A transport model does not replace the viscosity and diffusivities you give: they are the values at the feed, and each cell scales them by the ratio of the model's value at its state to that at the feed. Give none and they are the model's own values at the feed. So a preset keeps the properties of its paper at the feed, and a design variable or history match on the viscosity or the diffusivities keeps its meaning:

julia
gas = Mocca.krishnamurthy_2014_gas(transport = :chapman_enskog)
process = Mocca.lpp_vsa_process(gas = gas)

In a process file, set "thermodynamics": "polynomial" and "transport": "chapman_enskog" in the gas (see Process files).

The adsorbed phase takes the heat capacities of the gas, weighted by the loadings. The heat capacity ratio of pumps and compressors is that of the feed, unless the process sets heat_capacity_ratio (the presets set 1.4, as their papers do).

Against the NIST Chemistry WebBook, from 300 to 500 K: the largest deviation of the cubic heat capacities, and the deviation of the Chapman–Enskog viscosity from that of the gas at 0.01 bar.

Water is polar, and a Lennard-Jones potential describes it poorly: its viscosity is 10% high at 300 K. Since each cell takes the ratio to the feed, that offset cancels, but the temperature trend of water's viscosity is too steep. The Shomate coefficients match the NIST sets exactly. That of water is fitted from 500 K, but is within 0.05% of the NIST heat capacity of the vapour from 300 to 400 K. Those of N₂ and O₂ are valid only to 500 and 700 K.

At 25 °C and 1 atm the binary diffusivities are 1.55×10⁻⁵ m²/s for CO₂–N₂ (Mocca's constant default is 1.6×10⁻⁵), 2.14×10⁻⁵ for N₂–H₂O and 1.48×10⁻⁵ for CO₂–H₂O.

Limits ​

  • The diffusivity of the linear driving force is taken at the feed pressure; only its temperature and composition change. Molecular diffusion goes as , but at the 0.03 bar of an evacuation that makes mass transfer about 30 times faster than at 1 bar, and the cyclic steady state of the wet flue gas processes does not converge. In the macropores of a pellet, Knudsen diffusion would limit that rise; Mocca does not model it. The axial dispersion takes the diffusivities at the pressure of each cell.

  • Wilke's mixture diffusivity has a floor of 10⁻⁶ on the mole fractions of the other components (see Transport properties). Without it, near a pure component in a gas of three or more, Newton's method fails. The floor changes no weight by more than 1% for mole fractions above 10⁻⁴, and a binary gas gets its binary diffusivity exactly.

  • Wakao and Funazkri's molecular term, , is large: at 1 bar it is comparable to the velocity term, and at evacuation pressures it dominates the dispersion. Scale it with the bed's DispersionScale parameter, or give dispersion to add_bed! to fix the dispersion.

  • Thermal conductivity stays as given: the bed's axial conductivity is one effective value for gas and solid, and the wall's conductivity and the heat transfer coefficients are constant.

The heat capacities change little. Nearly all of the drop of the wet flue gas processes is the mass transfer of water: its diffusivity in the CO₂-rich gas of an evacuation is 0.75 of that in the feed, so water is taken up more slowly, its front spreads further into the zeolite and takes CO₂ capacity. With only the viscosity following the state, the LPP process recovers 0.793.
