Getting started ​

This example goes through a whole study of a process: read it from Mocca's library, change its operating settings, run it to cyclic steady state, measure its performance, look at the results, export them to a spreadsheet, save the process to a file to edit and share, and check that the cycle is steady.

The process is the four-step vacuum swing adsorption (VSA) cycle of Haghpanah et al. (2013), which captures CO₂ from a flue gas of 15% CO₂ in N₂ on zeolite 13X:

  • Pressurisation: the top of the bed is closed and feed enters at the bottom, bringing the bed up to the feed pressure.

  • Adsorption: feed enters the bottom; CO₂ is adsorbed and nitrogen leaves the top.

  • Blowdown: the bottom is closed and the bed is taken down to an intermediate pressure through the top, which removes most of the nitrogen.

  • Evacuation: the top is closed and the bed is evacuated through the bottom, which gives the CO₂ product.

julia
import Mocca
using Mocca.Units

1. Read a process from the library ​

The library holds the processes and adsorbents of published studies:

julia
println(Mocca.library_processes())
println(Mocca.library_adsorbents())
["haghpanah_2013_four_stage_vsa", "krishnamurthy_2014_dual_adsorbent_vsa", "krishnamurthy_2014_lpp_vsa"]
["silica_gel_krishnamurthy_2014", "zeolite_13x_haghpanah_2013", "zeolite_13x_krishnamurthy_2014"]

A process holds everything needed to run it: the gas and feed, the adsorbent and column, the flowsheet of beds and devices, the cycle of steps, the time stepping and the design variables.

julia
process = Mocca.read_process("haghpanah_2013_four_stage_vsa")
Process Haghpanah et al. (2013), four-step VSA: CO2/N2 feed at 1.0 bar, 298.15 K, capturing CO2
  Bed: fixed bed of zeolite 13X, 30 cells
    bottom V_feed (feed), from boundary at 1.0 bar; V_vacuum (extract), to boundary at 0.1 bar
    top    V_product (product), to boundary at 1.0 bar
  cycle of 4 stages, 100.0 s:
    pressurisation                        15.0 s   V_feed
    adsorption                            15.0 s   V_feed, V_product
    blowdown                              30.0 s   V_product
    evacuation                            40.0 s   V_vacuum
  variables: t_ads, t_bd, t_evac, P_I, P_L, v_feed

2. Change the operating settings ​

The design variables of the process are named settings. Setting one changes every place it acts: the evacuation pressure P_L is also where the next pressurisation starts. Values are in SI units; Mocca.Units has conversions such as bar.

julia
Mocca.variable_names(process)
6-element Vector{Symbol}:
 :t_ads
 :t_bd
 :t_evac
 :P_I
 :P_L
 :v_feed
julia
process[:P_L] = 0.05bar
process[:t_ads] = 30.0
process[:P_L], process[:t_ads]
(5000.0, 30.0)

The cycle is compiled into stages, each a set of device settings:

julia
for st in Mocca.stages(process)
    println(rpad(st.name, 16), st.duration, " s, opens ", join(keys(st.settings), ", "))
end
pressurisation  15.0 s, opens V_feed
adsorption      30.0 s, opens V_feed, V_product
blowdown        30.0 s, opens V_product
evacuation      40.0 s, opens V_vacuum

3. Run it to cyclic steady state ​

cyclic_steady_state repeats the cycle until the state of the bed at the end of a cycle is the state it started from, accelerating the approach with Newton's method. The result holds the steady cycle.

julia
result = Mocca.cyclic_steady_state(process)
println("converged: $(result.converged), cycles: $(result.iterations)")
converged: true, cycles: 24

4. Measure its performance ​

Purity and recovery of CO₂ in the extract (the vacuum line), the pump and compressor energy per tonne of CO₂, and the productivity per m³ of adsorbent:

julia
perf = Mocca.performance(result)
println("purity $(round(100perf.purity, digits = 1))%, recovery $(round(100perf.recovery, digits = 1))%, ",
    "$(round(perf.specific_energy, digits = 1)) kWh/t, productivity $(round(perf.productivity, sigdigits = 3)) mol/(m³·s)")
purity 88.4%, recovery 64.3%, 126.9 kWh/t, productivity 0.684 mol/(m³·s)

5. Look at the results ​

The flow and composition through each device over the cycle, with the stages shaded. Flow is positive from a device's inlet to its outlet, so the vacuum line is positive while it draws gas out of the bed.

julia
stages = Mocca.stages(process)
f_streams = Mocca.plot_flowsheet_streams(result.states, process.model, result.timesteps; stages = stages)

Mole fractions and loadings along the bed at the end of each stage. Most of the CO₂ is held near the feed end, at the bottom. Blowdown removes most of the nitrogen, and at the end of evacuation the gas in the bed is nearly pure CO₂.

julia
bed = process.flowsheet[:Bed]
f_profiles = Mocca.plot_profiles(result.states, result.timesteps, bed, cumsum([st.duration for st in stages]);
    labels = [st.name for st in stages], unit = :Bed)

Each state in result.states holds the state of every bed and device after a time step, in SI units, for any other quantity. For example, the range of the bed temperature over the cycle:

julia
T_min = minimum(minimum(s[:Bed][:Temperature]) for s in result.states)
T_max = maximum(maximum(s[:Bed][:Temperature]) for s in result.states)
println("bed temperature from $(round(T_min - 273.15, digits = 1)) to $(round(T_max - 273.15, digits = 1)) °C")
bed temperature from 20.0 to 28.4 °C

6. Export to a spreadsheet ​

write_results writes the result to a folder of CSV files that open in a spreadsheet, in bara, °C, mol%, kg/h, Nm³/h and mol/kg, with the units in the column headers. It also writes the process, to rerun it later.

julia
dir = joinpath(mktempdir(), "base_case")
files = Mocca.write_results(dir, result)
foreach(println, basename.(files))
summary.csv
streams.csv
stage_streams.csv
stream_history.csv
bed_history.csv
bed_profiles.csv

7. Save the process, edit it and read it back ​

A process file holds every layer of the process and reads back as the same process:

julia
file = Mocca.write_process(joinpath(mktempdir(), "vsa.json"), process)
same = Mocca.read_process(file)
same[:P_L] / bar
0.05

Process files can be written by hand. Numbers are SI, or strings with a unit, such as "0.1 bar" or "25 °C". Compositions and per-component values are keyed by component name, steps by their type, and an adsorbent may be the name of a library adsorbent. The Process files page describes the format.

julia
text = """
{
  "name": "a short cycle",
  "gas": {"components": ["CO2", "N2"], "molar_masses": {"N2": 0.028}, "heat_capacities": {"CO2": 697.5687, "N2": 1096.4}},
  "feed": {"composition": {"CO2": 0.15, "N2": 0.85}, "pressure": "1 bar", "temperature": "25 °C"},
  "adsorbents": {"13X": "zeolite_13x_haghpanah_2013"},
  "columns": {"column": {"length": "1 m", "radius": "14.45 cm", "outer_radius": "16.2 cm", "porosity": 0.37}},
  "units": [{"name": "Bed", "adsorbent": "13X", "column": "column", "ncells": 30}],
  "devices": [
    {"name": "V_feed", "role": "feed", "at": ["Bed", "bottom"]},
    {"name": "V_product", "role": "product", "at": ["Bed", "top"]},
    {"name": "V_vacuum", "role": "extract", "at": ["Bed", "bottom"]}
  ],
  "cycle": {"steps": [
    {"type": "pressurise", "duration": "10 s"},
    {"type": "adsorb", "duration": "10 s", "velocity": "1 m/s"},
    {"type": "blowdown", "duration": "20 s", "to": "0.2 bar"},
    {"type": "evacuate", "duration": "30 s", "to": "0.1 bar"}
  ]},
  "time_stepping": {"max_dt": 1.0, "first_dt": 0.05}
}
"""
file = joinpath(mktempdir(), "short.json")
write(file, text)
short = Mocca.read_process(file)

Mistakes are reported with where they are. A blowdown that goes up in pressure:

julia
try
    write(file, replace(text, "\"to\": \"0.2 bar\"" => "\"to\": \"2 bar\""))
    Mocca.read_process(file)
catch e
    println(sprint(showerror, e))
end
MoccaInputError in step 3 (blowdown) of the cycle, to: the step should lower the pressure of Bed, but goes from 1.0 bar to 2.0 bar

8. Check the steady state ​

simulate runs a given number of cycles from a given state, rather than to steady state. Started from the start of the steady cycle, result.state0, the bed comes back to the same state at the end of every cycle. The largest relative change of any variable over three cycles:

julia
result_cycles = Mocca.simulate(process; cycles = 3, state0 = result.state0)
Mocca.cycle_change(process.model, result.state0, result_cycles.states[end])
2.3119849011322536e-7

The top of the bed, where the nitrogen product leaves, over the three cycles:

julia
bed_states = [s[:Bed] for s in result_cycles.states]
f_outlet = Mocca.plot_cell(bed_states, bed, result_cycles.timesteps, 30)
