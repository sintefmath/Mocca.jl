Processes and validation ​

Mocca's library holds processes from published studies, set up at the papers' operating conditions. Read one by name with read_process, change a setting and run it:

julia
using Mocca
library_processes()
process = read_process("krishnamurthy_2014_lpp_vsa")

This page compares Mocca's results for each of them with the paper's, and says where they differ and why, as far as it is known.

How Mocca compares ​

Mocca's results against the published ones. A point on the diagonal is an exact match; the dotted lines are ±10%.

Four-step VSApaper88.7%35.8%
Mocca79.5%36.7%
Wet flue gas, LPPpaper78.8% (95.2% dry)88.6%188.8 kWh/t1.73 t/(m³·day)

Wet flue gas, dual adsorbentpaper95%90%177 kWh/t1.82 t/(m³·day)
Mocca95.6%85.6%192 kWh/t1.74 t/(m³·day)

The energy counts the vacuum pumps only, as the papers do; performance also counts the feed compressor. Productivity is per m³ of 13X. Purities with water count the water in the CO₂ product.

What is known about the differences:

  • Four-step VSA. Mocca's purity is about 9 points below the paper's at the same recovery, and the difference does not change with the grid, the time step or the heat model. Its cause has not been found yet.

  • Wet flue gas, LPP. Water builds up in the bed over hundreds of cycles, and after the 300 cycles of the example the CO₂ balance is still 0.7% open. Mocca pressurises with nitrogen where the paper uses stored light product. Recovery is 9 points below the paper's.

  • Wet flue gas, dual adsorbent. The CO₂ balance closes after 131 cycles, but the silica gel is still filling with water, and in Mocca a few hundred ppm of water reach the 13X bed, against 20 ppm in the paper. Recovery is 4 points lower and the energy 8% higher.

Four-step VSA ​

Haghpanah et al. (2013): dry flue gas, one bed of zeolite 13X. Library name "haghpanah_2013_four_stage_vsa".

Pressures1 bar adsorption, 0.2 bar blowdown, 0.1 bar evacuation
Stepspressurisation 15 s, adsorption 15 s, blowdown 30 s, evacuation 40 s

The Getting started example runs it, and How a VSA cycle works follows it through one cycle.

Two-bed VSA ​

Built in Mocca from the beds of Haghpanah et al. (2013), to show a multi-bed cycle; it has no published counterpart. Make it with two_bed_vsa_process().

Evacuating to 0.1 bar, it gives 57.4% purity, 60.6% recovery and 93.4 kWh/t, pumps and compressor together. The Two-bed VSA with pressure equalisation example builds it step by step and shows what the equalisation is worth.

Wet flue gas, dual adsorbent ​

Section 4 of Krishnamurthy et al. (2014): a silica gel bed takes the water out of the wet flue gas before a 13X bed captures the CO₂. Library name "krishnamurthy_2014_dual_adsorbent_vsa".

SettingValue

Columnssilica gel 0.41 m long, 13X 1 m long, both 0.3 m inner diameter

Stepssilica gel 154 s and then closed until the 13X bed finishes its 224 s

The Wet flue gas: dual-adsorbent, two-bed VSA example runs it and follows the water into the 13X bed.
