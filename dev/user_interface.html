Reference ​

The functions and types for setting up, running and measuring a process. A process is described in layers, from the physics to the operation. Each layer is a plain object that can be edited, saved and reused on its own:

LayerObjects
GasGas, Feed, Component
MaterialsAdsorbent with an isotherm by component (dual_site_langmuir, langmuir), Column, Wall
FlowsheetFlowsheet, beds (add_bed!) and devices with roles (add_device!)
Operationa Cycle of steps (Pressurise, Adsorb, Blowdown, Evacuate, Purge, Equalise, Idle, Concurrent, or your own), TimeStepping
ProcessProcess: simulate, cyclic_steady_state, performance
Designadd_variable!, performance_gradient, optimise!
Filesread_process, write_process, load_adsorbent; see Process files
ResultsProcessResult, write_results, plots; see Results and plots

The Building a process from its parts example uses each layer in turn. To add your own kinds of step or unit, see Extending Mocca.

Units ​

Everything is in plain SI units: Pa, K, m, s, mol, kg and J.

Mocca.Units Module
julia
Mocca.Units

Mocca works in plain SI units throughout: Pa, K, m, s, mol, kg and J. These constants convert other units to SI by multiplication, so 0.1bar is 1e4 (Pa) and -36.6kJ_per_mol is -36600.0 (J/mol):

julia
using Mocca.Units
Evacuate(40.0; to = 0.1bar)

Units.celsius converts a temperature in °C to K. Files read with read_process may give a number with one of these units as a string, such as "0.1 bar".

Mocca.Interface.Component Type
julia
Component(name; formula = name, cas = "", molar_mass, heat_capacity, viscosity,
    shomate = nothing, cp_polynomial = nothing, lennard_jones = nothing)

A chemical component: its molar mass [kg/mol], gas heat capacity [J/(kg·K)] and gas viscosity [Pa·s], both near 25 °C, and optionally:

  • shomate: the coefficients (A, B, C, D, E) of the Shomate equation for its molar heat capacity (see IdealGasShomate);

  • cp_polynomial: the coefficients (A, B, C, D) of its molar heat capacity as a cubic of temperature (see IdealGasPolynomial);

  • lennard_jones: its Lennard-Jones collision diameter σ [Å] and well depth ε/k [K], (σ, ε/k), for viscosity and diffusivity by the kinetic theory of gases (see ChapmanEnskogTransport).

Mocca knows common gases by name (see component); add others with register_component!.

Mocca.Interface.Gas Type
julia
Gas(names; molar_masses, heat_capacities, thermodynamics, shomate, viscosity,
    density, diffusivity, transport = :constant)

A gas mixture of the named components, in order. The order sets the order of compositions and loadings everywhere; by convention the first component is the one to capture (it is the key component of purity and recovery).

Properties default to those of the components (see component), and can be overridden, all at once as a vector in gas order or for some components by name, such as heat_capacities = (CO2 = 697.6,):

  • molar_masses [kg/mol] per component;

  • the heat capacities, through the thermodynamics of the gas (see AbstractGasThermodynamics):

    • by default constant, heat_capacities [J/(kg·K)] per component (IdealGasConstantCp);

    • thermodynamics = :polynomial: cubic in temperature, with the cp_polynomial coefficients of the components (IdealGasPolynomial);

    • thermodynamics = :shomate: by the Shomate equation (IdealGasShomate), with the coefficients of the components, or shomate, coefficients (A, B, C, D, E) per component. Giving shomate chooses this;

    • or an AbstractGasThermodynamics model for the components in gas order.

    The heat capacity of the mixture is the mole average of the molar heat capacities, ;

  • transport: how the viscosity and diffusivities change from their values at the feed with the temperature, pressure and composition of each cell of a bed (see AbstractGasTransport). :constant (the default) keeps them; :chapman_enskog follows the kinetic theory of gases, with the Lennard-Jones parameters of the components (ChapmanEnskogTransport); or give an AbstractGasTransport model for the components in gas order. With a transport model a bed's axial dispersion follows the diffusivities and the gas velocity, by Wakao and Funazkri, unless add_bed! is given a dispersion;

  • viscosity [Pa·s] of the mixture at the feed. By default that of the transport model at the feed, or with constant transport the Herning–Zipperer mixture of the component viscosities at 25 °C;

  • density [kg/m³] of the mixture, by default the ideal gas at the feed pressure and temperature. The built-in units do not use it;

  • diffusivity [m²/s]: molecular diffusivity at the feed, one value or one per component. It sets the macropore diffusion of the default linear driving force and the axial dispersion. By default that of the transport model at the feed, or with constant transport 1.6e-5 m²/s, that of CO2 in N2 at 25 °C and 1 atm.

gas.heat_capacities holds the heat capacities [J/(kg·K)] per component at 25 °C, gas.thermodynamics the thermodynamics model and gas.transport the transport model.

The heat capacities of a gas come from its thermodynamics model: constant by default, as given by heat_capacities per mass, or changing with temperature with thermodynamics = :shomate:

julia
gas = Gas(["CO2", "N2", "H2O"]; thermodynamics = :shomate)
molar_heat_capacity(gas.thermodynamics, [0.15, 0.82, 0.03], 350.0)  # J/(mol·K)
Mocca.Physics.AbstractGasThermodynamics Type
julia
AbstractGasThermodynamics

Abstract base type for thermodynamic models of a gas mixture: the heat capacity and enthalpy of each component, and the equation of state.

Implementations must provide:

  • component_heat_capacities(model, T): molar heat capacity of each component [J/(mol·K)] at temperature T [K], as an SVector.

and may provide:

  • component_enthalpies(model, T): molar enthalpy of each component [J/mol] relative to THERMODYNAMIC_REFERENCE_TEMPERATURE; by default the heat capacities integrated by Gauss–Legendre quadrature;

  • molar_concentration(model, P, T): by default that of the ideal gas. The energy balances of Mocca assume an ideal gas.

The mixture properties (molar_heat_capacity, molar_enthalpy, heat_capacity_ratio) follow from these. Every function must work for automatic-differentiation numbers and not allocate, as it is evaluated in every cell at every Newton iteration.

Mocca.Physics.AbstractGasTransport Type
julia
AbstractGasTransport

Abstract base type for models of the transport properties of a gas mixture: its viscosity and the molecular diffusivity of each component, as they change with temperature, pressure and composition.

A unit holds such a model with its values at the state at which the unit's viscosity and diffusivities are given (see TransportReference), and scales those by the ratio of the model's value at the state of a cell to its value there. The given values keep their meaning, and stay what design variables and gradients act on.

Implementations must provide:

  • mixture_viscosity(model, y, T) [Pa·s];

  • molecular_diffusivities(model, y, T, P) [m²/s]: the diffusivity of each component in the mixture, as an SVector.

As for AbstractGasThermodynamics, each must work for automatic-differentiation numbers and not allocate.

source

Mocca.Physics.ChapmanEnskogTransport Type
julia
ChapmanEnskogTransport{N, T} <: AbstractGasTransport

Transport properties of a mixture of ideal gases by the kinetic theory of Chapman and Enskog, with the Lennard-Jones parameters of each component and the collision integrals of Neufeld et al. (Poling, Prausnitz and O'Connell, The Properties of Gases and Liquids, 5th ed., §9-3, §9-5 and §11-3):

  • viscosity of each component, μᵢ = 2.6693e-6·√(Mᵢ T) / (σᵢ² Ω_μ(T/εᵢ)) [Pa·s], with Mᵢ in g/mol and σᵢ in Å, mixed by Wilke, μ = Σᵢ yᵢ μᵢ / Σⱼ yⱼ Φᵢⱼ, Φᵢⱼ = [1 + (μᵢ/μⱼ)^(1/2) (Mⱼ/Mᵢ)^(1/4)]² / [8 (1 + Mᵢ/Mⱼ)]^(1/2);

  • binary diffusivities, Dᵢⱼ = 1.858e-7·T^(3/2)·√(1/Mᵢ + 1/Mⱼ) / (P σᵢⱼ² Ω_D(T/εᵢⱼ)) [m²/s], with P in atm, σᵢⱼ = (σᵢ + σⱼ)/2 and εᵢⱼ = √(εᵢ εⱼ), and the diffusivity of each component in the mixture by Wilke, Dᵢ = Σ_{j≠i} yⱼ / Σ_{j≠i} (yⱼ/Dᵢⱼ). ChapmanEnskogTransport(; sigma, epsilon, molar_masses)

with one value per component of each: collision diameter sigma [Å], well depth epsilon as ε/k [K], and molar mass [kg/mol].

Mocca.Physics.WakaoFunazkri Type
julia
WakaoFunazkri(; particle_diameter, porosity, diffusivities) <: AbstractAxialDispersion

Axial dispersion by the correlation of Wakao and Funazkri (1978),

julia
ε·D_ax,i / D_m,i = 20 + 0.5·Sc_i·Re_p,   Re_p = ρ u d_p / μ,   Sc_i = μ / (ρ D_m,i),

that is D_ax,i = (20 D_m,i + 0.5 |u| d_p) / ε, with u the superficial velocity [m/s], d_p the particle_diameter [m] and ε the bed porosity. D_m,i is the molecular diffusivity of component i: diffusivities [m²/s] at the feed, scaled by the unit's transport model (see TransportReference). The unit's DispersionScale parameter multiplies it (1 by default).

source
Mocca.Balances.DispersionScale Type

Isotherms, adsorbents and columns ​

Isotherm parameters are given by component name, so an adsorbent does not depend on the order of the components of a gas, and components can be added or removed without editing parallel arrays.

Mocca.Interface.AbstractIsothermData Type
julia
AbstractIsothermData

Isotherm parameters given by component name, independent of the order of the components in a gas. build_isotherm turns them into an AbstractIsotherm for a particular Gas. Components of the gas without parameters do not adsorb; parameters for components the gas does not have are ignored, so one adsorbent can serve gases with different components.

Implement build_isotherm(data, gas; density) for a new kind, and register it with register_isotherm! to read and write it in files.

source
Mocca.Interface.dual_site_langmuir Function
julia
dual_site_langmuir(name => parameters, ...; reference_temperature = 298.15, basis = :volume)

Dual-site Langmuir isotherm data, one entry per component:

julia
dual_site_langmuir(
    "CO2" => (qsb = 3489.44, b0 = 8.65e-7, ΔUb = -36.64kJ_per_mol, qsd = 2872.35, d0 = 2.63e-8, ΔUd = -35.69kJ_per_mol),
    "N2" => (qsb = 6613.55, b0 = 2.50e-6, ΔUb = -15.8kJ_per_mol))

For each site, b (strong) and d (weak): saturation capacity qsb, qsd; affinity at infinite temperature b0, d0 [m³/mol], on a concentration basis; and internal energy of adsorption ΔUb, ΔUd [J/mol], negative. Omitted d-site values are zero. dUb and dUd may be written for ΔUb and ΔUd.

Capacities are in mol per m³ of adsorbent with basis = :volume, or mol/kg with basis = :mass. reference_temperature [K] is where the isosteric heat of adsorption is evaluated.

Flowsheets ​

Devices have roles. A role sets the device's default boundary condition and valve size, tells the steps which device to open, and tells performance which streams are feed and product.

Mocca.Interface.Flowsheet Type
julia
Flowsheet(gas::Gas, feed::Feed)

An empty flowsheet for gas, fed with feed. Add beds with add_bed! and devices with add_device!, and run it as a Process.

Devices have roles (feed, product, extract, ...) that give their default boundary conditions and valve sizes, tell the step templates which device to open, and tell performance which streams are feed and product. The units and connections are a UnitNetwork, so the lower-level functions, such as add_unit!, connect! and setup_flowsheet_model, work on a flowsheet too.

source
Mocca.Interface.add_bed! Function
julia
add_bed!(fs, name, adsorbent, column; ncells = 30, thermal = WithWall(),
    dispersion_velocity = :adsorption, dispersion = nothing, permeability = nothing)

Add a fixed bed of adsorbent in column to the flowsheet under name. Its ports are :bottom and :top.

The axial dispersion depends on the gas velocity. By default (dispersion_velocity = :adsorption) it is set from the interstitial velocity of the bed's adsorption step when a Process is built, or the largest adsorption velocity in the cycle for a bed fed from another; it then stays fixed as settings change. Give a velocity [m/s], or the dispersion [m²/s] itself, to fix it. It is the bed's AxialDispersion parameter, so it can also be set through parameters of a Process or a design variable. With a transport model for the gas and no dispersion given, the dispersion instead follows the diffusivities and the gas velocity at each face, by WakaoFunazkri, times the bed's DispersionScale parameter, which it has in place of AxialDispersion. See fixed_bed_model for the other keywords.

source

Add a flow device with a role (see DEVICE_ROLES), connected to a bed port at = (bed, port), such as (:Bed, :bottom). A link joins from, a bed port, to to, another.

Cycles and steps ​

A step acts on a bed and opens the devices it needs, found by their roles; every other device stays closed. Each step that changes the pressure starts where the step before ended, wrapping around the cycle; give from to start elsewhere.

Mocca.Interface.Cycle Type
julia
Cycle(steps)
Cycle(label => steps, label => steps, ...; cycle_time = nothing)

How each stage is split into time steps: steps of at most max_dt [s], starting with first_dt and doubling, which carries the solver smoothly through the abrupt start of a stage (and keeps the cycle map smooth for Newton's method and gradients; see setup_schedule).

Mocca.Interface.Process Type
julia
Process(fs, cycle; name = "", timestepping = TimeStepping(), key_component = 1,
    ambient_pressure = 101325.0, pump_efficiency = 0.72, heat_capacity_ratio = nothing,
    ramp_rate = 0.5, initial_state = Dict(), parameters = Dict())

A cyclic adsorption process: the flowsheet fs (built with Flowsheet(gas, feed), add_bed! and add_device!) operated by cycle (see Cycle).

  • timestepping: see TimeStepping.

  • key_component: the component to capture, by index or name, for performance.

  • ambient_pressure [Pa]: vacuum pumps discharge to it and feed compressors take gas from it; pump_efficiency and heat_capacity_ratio are those of both. The heat capacity ratio is by default that of the feed, from the thermodynamics of the gas (see pump_heat_capacity_ratio).

  • ramp_rate [1/s]: of the pressure ramps of steps that do not set their own.

  • initial_state: per unit with holdup, a NamedTuple with any of pressure, temperature, wall_temperature and composition. By default each unit starts at the lowest pressure of its cycle (the feed pressure if no step sets it), at the feed temperature, full of light product, with the sorbent of a bed at equilibrium (see setup_unit_state).

  • parameters: per unit, values of its Jutul parameters to override, such as Dict(:Bed => Dict(:AdsorbentDensity => 1200.0)).

Simulate it with simulate, find its cyclic steady state with cyclic_steady_state, and measure it with performance.

The process keeps its own copies of fs and cycle, so changing them afterwards does not change the process, and several processes can be built from the same ones. Change a process through its design variables (see add_variable!); p.flowsheet and p.cycle are its copies.

Mocca.Interface.cyclic_steady_state Function
julia
cyclic_steady_state(p; method = :newton, tol = nothing, max_cycles = 500,
    warm_start = true, handover = 3, krylov = :auto, coarse_cells = 40,
    progress = isinteractive(), info_level = -1, kwarg...) → ProcessResult

The cyclic steady state of the process, with the states of its last cycle:

  • :newton (default): handover plain cycles (3), each from the end of the last, then Newton's method on the cycle map (see newton_cyclic_steady_state) to tol (default 1e-9). The plain cycles let the fastest modes die out, and Newton's method controls its steps, so it needs no closer start. A handover below 1 is a cycle change instead: Anderson acceleration until the change is below it. If Newton's method fails or does not converge, Anderson acceleration goes on to a change of 1e-1, and then if need be 1e-3, and Newton's method tries again from each. With warm_start, it starts from the last steady state found for this process, and its Jacobian, which is much faster when the settings have changed a little. With krylov = true, each Newton step is solved by GMRES instead of from the full Jacobian, which pays off for fine grids, where the variables far outnumber the slow modes of the cycle; false always builds the full Jacobian. The default, :auto, decides on each grid: GMRES when the beds have more than 800 variables (a bed of about 130 cells with two components, 100 with three), the full Jacobian below; krylov = n sets that number of variables to n. On a fine grid, with a bed of more than coarse_cells cells, the steady state is found first on beds of half as many cells (and so on, down to coarse_cells or fewer), each interpolated onto the next finer grid as the start of Newton's method there, which then needs only a few iterations; a coarser grid is converged to 1e-6 only. Starting fine grids cold is slow, and on some processes (such as the wet LPP VSA from about 80 cells) Newton's method does not converge from a few plain cycles. The coarser grids only give the start: the steady state is that of p. If Newton's method does not converge from the coarser grid's steady state, p is started from its initial state as above. Pass coarse_cells = nothing to start from the initial state on any grid.

  • :anderson: simulate_to_cyclic_steady_state to tol (default 1e-6).

  • :mass_balance: simulate_until_mass_balance on the key component, between the feed and light inlet devices and the outlet devices.

With progress, on by default in an interactive session such as the REPL, each cycle is printed as it finishes: how many have run, how much the state still changes over a cycle against tol, a bar of how far it has come, and an estimate of the cycles and time left. In a terminal the line is rewritten in place, so only the end of each phase stays on screen. progress can also be an IO to print to, or a CycleProgress.

source
Mocca.Interface.performance Function
julia
performance(r::ProcessResult) → NamedTuple

Performance of the process over the last cycle of r, from the streams through its devices (by their roles; see add_device!):

  • purity: mole fraction of the key component in the extract;

  • recovery: fraction of the key component fed that leaves in the extract;

  • specific_energy [kWh per tonne of key component in the extract]: vacuum pumps on every outlet device whose gas leaves below ambient pressure, and compressors on every inlet device whose gas enters a bed above it, as in Haghpanah et al. (2013);

  • productivity [mol of key component per m³ of adsorbent per s];

  • energy [J per cycle], cycle_time [s], the component totals [mol per cycle] of the feed, extract and light product, and the adsorbent_volume [m³] and adsorbent_mass [kg].

julia
performance_gradient(p; variables = all, steady_state = nothing, rtol = 1e-7,

The performance of the process at cyclic steady state and its gradient with respect to the design variables (names; see add_variable!), by adjoints and the implicit function theorem (see cyclic_steady_state_gradient). Mocca.Interface.optimise! Function
julia
optimise!(p; variables = all, maximise = nothing, minimise = nothing,
    at_least = (;), at_most = (;), linear_constraints = [], scale = (;),
    weight = 1e3, max_it = 25, log = nothing, print_level = 0) → NamedTuple

Optimise the process at cyclic steady state with gradients (BFGS in the box of the variables' bounds, Jutul.unit_box_bfgs): maximise or minimise one performance measure, such as maximise = :recovery, subject to others held above or below targets, such as at_least = (purity = 0.9,). Measure constraints are quadratic penalties with weight on the shortfall in units of scale (per measure, 1 by default), so the result may miss them slightly. linear_constraints are (Dict(name => coefficient, ...), bound) pairs, meaning Σ coefficient·variable ≤ bound, such as (Dict(:P_L => 1.0, :P_I => -1.0), -0.01bar) for P_I ≥ P_L + 0.01 bar.

Variables whose bounds are equal stay fixed. Each evaluation finds the cyclic steady state from the last one. log(values, result) is called after each. Returns the best values (a NamedTuple), its metrics, the number of evaluations and the history of every evaluation, and leaves the process set to the best values.
