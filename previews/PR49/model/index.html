Model equations

A fixed bed in Mocca is a one-dimensional, axially dispersed plug flow model of a packed column, as in Haghpanah et al. (2013). The gas is ideal, flow follows Darcy's law, mass transfer to the sorbent is a linear driving force, and the energy balance covers the gas, the sorbent and the column wall. The beds are connected to the feed, products and each other through flow devices (valves and flow controllers), whose settings change from step to step of the cycle.

All quantities are in SI units. The symbols are listed in the nomenclature at the end of this page, with the inputs they come from.

Fixed bed

Component mass balance

For each component $i$ of the gas,

\[\varepsilon \frac{\partial c_i}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial (u\, c_i)}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(\varepsilon D_L C \frac{\partial y_i}{\partial z}\right) + (1-\varepsilon)\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial t} = 0, \qquad c_i = y_i C = \frac{y_i P}{RT}.\]

$q_i$ is the loading in mol per m³ of adsorbent (particles), so $(1-\varepsilon)$ converts it to bed volume.

Momentum: Darcy's law

The superficial velocity follows Darcy's law with the Kozeny–Carman (Blake–Kozeny) permeability of a packed bed,

\[u = -\frac{k}{\mu}\frac{\partial P}{\partial z}, \qquad k = \frac{\varepsilon^3 d_p^2}{150\,(1-\varepsilon)^2}.\]

This is the viscous (laminar) term of the Ergun equation; the inertial term is not included. The interstitial velocity is $v = u/\varepsilon$. Give permeability to add_bed! to override $k$.

Axial dispersion

\[D_L = 0.7\, D_m + 0.5\, v_0\, d_p,\]

where $D_m$ is the molecular diffusivity of the gas (diffusivity of Gas, that of the first component if given per component) and $v_0$ the interstitial feed velocity of the bed's adsorption step. $D_L$ is evaluated once, when the process is built, and is constant along the bed and through the cycle. Set dispersion_velocity or dispersion in add_bed! to fix it yourself.

Adsorption rate: linear driving force

\[\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial t} = k_i \left(q_i^* - q_i\right), \qquad k_i = \frac{c_i}{q_i^*}\,\frac{15\,\varepsilon_p D_{m,i}}{\tau\, r_p^2}, \qquad r_p = \frac{d_p}{2}.\]

This is macropore diffusion control: the particle porosity $\varepsilon_p$, tortuosity $\tau$ and diameter $d_p$ of the Adsorbent set the rate.

Isotherm: dual-site Langmuir

\[q_i^* = \frac{q_{sb,i}\, b_i c_i}{1 + \sum_j b_j c_j} + \frac{q_{sd,i}\, d_i c_i}{1 + \sum_j d_j c_j}, \qquad b_i = b_{0,i}\, e^{-\Delta U_{b,i}/RT}, \quad d_i = d_{0,i}\, e^{-\Delta U_{d,i}/RT}.\]

The affinities are on a concentration basis (m³/mol), and the saturation capacities in mol per m³ of adsorbent, or in mol/kg with basis = :mass, which Mocca multiplies by the adsorbent density. The single-site langmuir isotherm has $q_{sd,i} = 0$. Check an isotherm against its source with equilibrium_loading and isotherm_table, which report mol/kg.

The heat of adsorption of each component is constant, the average of its two site energies weighted by their capacities at the reference temperature $T_0$ (reference_temperature, default 298.15 K):

\[\Delta H_i = \frac{q_{sb,i}\left(\Delta U_{b,i} - RT_0\right) + q_{sd,i}\left(\Delta U_{d,i} - RT_0\right)}{q_{sb,i} + q_{sd,i}}.\]

Energy balance of the column

Written in terms of temperature and pressure:

\[\begin{aligned} &\left[(1-\varepsilon)\left(\rho_s c_{ps} + \tilde c_{pa} \sum_i q_i\right)\right]\frac{\partial T}{\partial t} + \varepsilon\,\frac{\tilde c_{pg}}{R}\frac{\partial P}{\partial t} + \frac{\tilde c_{pg}}{R}\frac{\partial (uP)}{\partial z} - \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\left(K_z \frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\right) \\ &\qquad + (1-\varepsilon)\sum_i \left(\tilde c_{pa} T + \Delta H_i\right)\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial t} + \frac{2 h_{in}}{r_{in}}\left(T - T_w\right) = 0, \end{aligned}\]

with molar heat capacities of the gas and the adsorbed phase from the per-mass values of each component,

\[\tilde c_{pg} = \bar M \sum_i y_i\, c_{pg,i}, \qquad \tilde c_{pa} = \bar M \sum_i y_i\, c_{pa,i}, \qquad \bar M = \sum_i y_i M_i.\]

$\Delta H_i < 0$, so adsorption heats the bed.

Wall energy balance

\[\rho_w c_{pw}\frac{\partial T_w}{\partial t} = K_w \frac{\partial^2 T_w}{\partial z^2} + \frac{2 r_{in} h_{in}}{r_{out}^2 - r_{in}^2}\left(T - T_w\right) - \frac{2 r_{out} h_{out}}{r_{out}^2 - r_{in}^2}\left(T_w - T_a\right).\]

At both ends the wall conducts heat to the surroundings at $T_a$.

The thermal keyword of add_bed! chooses the energy model:

Flow devices and boundary conditions

Each end of a bed (:bottom at $z = 0$, :top at $z = L$) connects to one or more flow devices. A device has an inlet and an outlet port and carries a molar flow $F$ [mol/s], positive from inlet to outlet. The port attached to the bed takes the pressure, temperature and composition of the end cell. The other port is either another bed (a link) or a boundary at a prescribed pressure, temperature and composition. The flow enters or leaves the bed's end cell carrying the composition and enthalpy $F\,\tilde c_{pg} T$ of the upstream port.

In each step of the cycle every device is closed, a valve or a flow controller:

LawFlow
Closed$F = 0$
LinearValve$F = C_v\,(P_{in} - P_{out})\,\dfrac{P_{up}}{R\,T_{up}}$
VolumetricFlow (flow controller)$F = Q\,\dfrac{P_{out}}{R\,T_{in}}$

Here $C_v$ is the valve conductance [m³/(s·Pa)] and "up" is the upstream port. A feed given as an interstitial velocity $v$ is a flow controller with $Q = \varepsilon v A$.

By default a valve has the conductance of half a cell at the bed end it is attached to, $C_v = kA/(\mu\,\Delta z/2)$, so that it adds no more resistance than the bed itself and the bed end follows the pressure on the far side of the valve. Give conductance to a step to model a smaller valve.

During pressurisation, blowdown and evacuation, the pressure on the far side of the valve moves exponentially from where the step starts to where it ends:

\[P(t) = P_{end} + \left(P_{start} - P_{end}\right) e^{-\lambda t},\]

with $\lambda$ the ramp_rate (default 0.5 s⁻¹) of the step or the Process.

For the four-step VSA cycle this gives the boundary conditions of Haghpanah et al. (2013):

StepBottom ($z = 0$)Top ($z = L$)
Pressurisationfeed valve, $P$ ramps $P_L \to P_H$closed
Adsorptionfeed at velocity $v_{feed}$product valve at $P_H$
Blowdownclosedproduct valve, $P$ ramps $P_H \to P_I$
Evacuationvacuum valve, $P$ ramps $P_I \to P_L$closed

Performance

performance measures a process over its last simulated cycle, from the streams through its devices. The device roles (see add_device!) say which streams count. For the key component $k$ (the first component of the gas unless key_component is given to Process), with $N_i^{r}$ the moles of component $i$ that passed through all devices of role $r$ over the cycle:

\[\text{purity} = \frac{N_k^{extract}}{\sum_i N_i^{extract}}, \qquad \text{recovery} = \frac{N_k^{extract}}{N_k^{feed}}, \qquad \text{productivity} = \frac{N_k^{extract}}{V_{ads}\; t_{cycle}},\]

with $V_{ads}$ the volume of adsorbent, $(1-\varepsilon)$ times the bed volume, summed over the beds. Productivity is in mol/(m³ adsorbent · s); multiply by $M_k \cdot 86400/1000$ for tonnes per m³ per day.

The energy counts a vacuum pump on every outlet device (roles :product, :extract, :waste) while it discharges below ambient pressure, and a compressor on every inlet device (:feed, :light_inlet) while it delivers above ambient pressure. Both compress adiabatically between the ambient pressure $P_{amb}$ and the device's pressure $P$:

\[E = \int \frac{F R T}{\eta}\,\frac{\gamma}{\gamma - 1} \left[\left(\frac{P_{high}}{P_{low}}\right)^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} - 1\right] dt, \qquad \text{specific energy} = \frac{E}{N_k^{extract} M_k},\]

reported in kWh per tonne of key component. $P_{amb}$, $\eta$ and $\gamma$ are the ambient_pressure (101325 Pa), pump_efficiency (0.72) and heat_capacity_ratio (1.4) of the Process. Haghpanah et al. count the vacuum pumps only; vacuum_pump_energy gives the energy of one device, to report it their way.

Cyclic steady state

The cycle maps the state of the beds at its start, $x$, to the state at its end, $\Phi(x)$. cyclic_steady_state solves $x = \Phi(x)$:

methodHowConverged when
:newton (default)Anderson acceleration, then Newton's method on $x - \Phi(x)$change per cycle below tol = 10⁻⁹
:andersonRepeated cycles with Anderson accelerationchange per cycle below tol = 10⁻⁶
:mass_balanceRepeated cycleskey component in and out agree within 0.5% for 5 cycles in a row, after at least 50 (Haghpanah et al. 2013; Krishnamurthy et al. 2014)

The change per cycle is the largest relative change of any variable of any bed (see cycle_change). :newton and :anderson require every variable to settle, including slowly accumulating components such as water; a mass balance on one component does not.

Numerical method

Each bed is divided into ncells equal cells (30 by default). Fluxes between cells are two-point, with upwinded advection, and time stepping is fully implicit (backward Euler), solved by Newton's method with exact derivatives from automatic differentiation. Each step of the cycle starts with time steps of first_dt that double up to max_dt (see TimeStepping), which carries the solver through the abrupt start of a step. Gradients for optimisation come from the adjoint of the same discrete equations.
