Mocca

Mocca simulates cyclic adsorption processes for CO₂ capture, such as pressure and vacuum swing adsorption (PSA/VSA). It finds the cyclic steady state of a process, measures its purity, recovery, energy use and productivity, and computes how these change with every operating setting, for optimisation. It is written in Julia and built on the Jutul simulator.

Quick start

Read the four-step VSA process of Haghpanah et al. (2013) from Mocca's library, lower its evacuation pressure, run it to cyclic steady state and measure it:

using Mocca
using Mocca.Units

process = read_process("haghpanah_2013_four_stage_vsa")
process[:P_L] = 0.05bar                  # evacuation pressure
result = cyclic_steady_state(process)
perf = performance(result)
julia> perf.purity, perf.recovery, perf.specific_energy
(0.7900223577190472, 0.7088768107066176, 149.85070321200155)

That is 79.0% CO₂ purity, 70.9% recovery and 150 kWh per tonne of CO₂. The first run in a session takes a few minutes while Julia compiles; after that, changing a setting and finding the new steady state takes seconds.

What Mocca models

  • Beds: one-dimensional, axially dispersed plug flow through a packed column, with Darcy's law for the pressure drop, a linear driving force for mass transfer, and an energy balance including the column wall (or an adiabatic or isothermal bed). See Model equations.
  • Isotherms: multicomponent dual-site and single-site Langmuir, with a library of published adsorbents (zeolite 13X, silica gel).
  • Flowsheets: any number of beds connected to the feed, the products and each other through valves and flow controllers.
  • Cycles: pressurisation (with feed or light product), adsorption, blowdown, evacuation, purge, pressure equalisation and idle steps, run in sequence or side by side in several beds.
  • Design: gradients of the performance at cyclic steady state with respect to step durations, pressures, flow rates and model parameters, and gradient-based optimisation.
  • Ready-made processes: the four-step VSA of Haghpanah et al. (2013), a two-bed VSA with pressure equalisation, and the wet flue gas processes of Krishnamurthy et al. (2014).

Not yet included: temperature swing (heated or cooled steps), the inertial (Ergun) term of the pressure drop, and other isotherm models (new ones can be added with register_isotherm!).
