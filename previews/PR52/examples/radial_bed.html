A column with a cooled wall, across its radius ​

A 1D bed has one temperature across its cross-section, and loses heat to the wall through a coefficient that lumps the resistance of the whole bed. Where the bed conducts heat poorly compared with how fast the wall takes it, the centre of a column runs hotter than the gas at the wall during adsorption, and a 1D bed cannot show it. This example meshes the bed across its radius as well, in rings from the axis to the wall, for a breakthrough into a column with a cooled wall, and shows when the 1D bed is enough.

julia
import Mocca
using Mocca.Units
using CairoMakie

The column ​

CO₂ and N₂ (15/85) on zeolite 13X, as in the breakthrough example, in the column of Haghpanah et al. (2013), here with a wall cooled from outside: a large outer coefficient keeps the wall near the ambient 25 °C. A bed meshed across its radius needs two coefficients a 1D bed lumps together: the effective radial conductivity of the packing, , and the coefficient of the film of gas at the wall, . The values here are for illustration.

julia
gas = Mocca.haghpanah_2013_gas()
feed = Mocca.Feed(gas; composition = (CO2 = 0.15, N2 = 0.85), temperature = 298.15, pressure = 1bar)
adsorbent = Mocca.load_adsorbent("zeolite_13x_haghpanah_2013")
column(K_r) = Mocca.Column(; length = 1.0, radius = 0.1445, outer_radius = 0.162, porosity = 0.37,
    axial_conductivity = 0.0903, outer_heat_transfer = 500.0, radial_conductivity = K_r, wall_heat_transfer = 30.0)
column (generic function with 1 method)

How much the temperature varies across the radius is set by the Biot number of the cross-section,  : well below 1 the cross-section is nearly at one temperature, well above it the wall cools only the gas next to it.

julia
biot(c) = c.wall_heat_transfer * c.radius / c.radial_conductivity
biot(column(0.2))
21.674999999999997

A breakthrough on rings ​

The same breakthrough on a 1D bed and on 30 cells along the column, each split into 6 rings. The wall cools a layer of the bed next to it that is thin at this Biot number, so the rings are narrower towards the wall: their edges, from the axis, are radii. The 1D bed takes the wall coefficient that gives the mean temperature of the bed across its radius,    (see Model equations).

julia
lumped(c) = 1 / (1 / c.wall_heat_transfer + c.radius / (4 * c.radial_conductivity))
radii = 0.1445 .* [0.0, 0.4, 0.65, 0.8, 0.9, 0.96, 1.0]

function breakthrough(c; radial_cells = 1)
    if radial_cells == 1
        c = Mocca.Column(; length = c.length, radius = c.radius, outer_radius = c.outer_radius, porosity = c.porosity,
            axial_conductivity = c.axial_conductivity, outer_heat_transfer = c.outer_heat_transfer,
            inner_heat_transfer = lumped(c))
    end
    fs = Mocca.Flowsheet(gas, feed)
    Mocca.add_bed!(fs, :Bed, adsorbent, c; ncells = 30, radial_cells = radial_cells,
        radii = radial_cells > 1 ? radii : nothing)
    Mocca.add_device!(fs, :V_feed, :feed; at = (:Bed, :bottom))
    Mocca.add_device!(fs, :V_product, :product; at = (:Bed, :top))
    p = Mocca.Process(fs, Mocca.Cycle([Mocca.Adsorb(1200.0; velocity = 0.5)]);
        timestepping = Mocca.TimeStepping(max_dt = 10.0, first_dt = 0.5),
        initial_state = Dict(:Bed => (pressure = 1bar, temperature = 298.15, composition = [1e-6, 1 - 1e-6])))
    return p, Mocca.simulate(p)
end

p1, r1 = breakthrough(column(0.2))
p6, r6 = breakthrough(column(0.2); radial_cells = 6)
bed = p6.flowsheet[:Bed]

The gas leaving the bed is the rings at the top mixed. On rings it breaks through a little later and leaves cooler: the gas next to the cooled wall stays cold, and the sorbent there takes up more CO₂ before the front passes.

julia
outlet(p, r) = [Mocca.port_state(p.flowsheet[:Bed], s[:Bed], :top) for s in r.states]
f_outlet = Figure(size = (800, 350))
ax_y = Axis(f_outlet[1, 1], xlabel = "t [s]", ylabel = "CO₂ at the outlet [-]")
ax_T = Axis(f_outlet[1, 2], xlabel = "t [s]", ylabel = "Temperature at the outlet [K]")
for (label, p, r) in (("1D bed", p1, r1), ("30 × 6 rings", p6, r6))
    t = cumsum(r.timesteps)
    lines!(ax_y, t, [o.composition[1] for o in outlet(p, r)], label = label)
    lines!(ax_T, t, [o.temperature for o in outlet(p, r)], label = label)
end
axislegend(ax_y, position = :lt)
f_outlet

Inside the bed the two differ more. 400 s in, the heat of adsorption has warmed the bed behind the front, and the wall has cooled the gas next to it, but not the centre:

julia
k = findlast(<=(400.0), cumsum(r6.timesteps))
f_section = Mocca.plot_bed_section(r6.states[k], bed; unit = :Bed, radial_scale = 2)

Across the radius, at a quarter and half way up the bed:

julia
f_radial = Mocca.plot_radial_profiles(r6.states, r6.timesteps, bed, [200.0, 400.0, 800.0];
    unit = :Bed, positions = [0.25, 0.5])

The heat leaves through a layer next to the wall, which the narrow outer rings resolve. At its most, the centre runs this much hotter than the outer ring [K]:

julia
centre_minus_wall(r) = maximum(maximum(s[:Bed][:Temperature][1:30] .- s[:Bed][:Temperature][151:180]) for s in r.states)
centre_minus_wall(r6)
48.06638730482524

When the 1D bed is enough ​

As the radial conductivity rises, the Biot number falls and the cross-section approaches one temperature. The hottest mean temperature of the cross-section half way up the bed, on rings and for the 1D bed with the lumped coefficient, and the largest difference between the centre and the outer ring:

julia
peak(p, r) = maximum(Mocca.axial_profile(p.flowsheet[:Bed], s[:Bed][:Temperature])[15] for s in r.states)
for K_r in (0.2, 1.0, 10.0)
    c = column(K_r)
    q1, s1 = breakthrough(c)
    q6, s6 = breakthrough(c; radial_cells = 6)
    println("K_r = ", rpad(K_r, 5), " Bi = ", rpad(round(biot(c), digits = 1), 5),
        " rings ", round(peak(q6, s6), digits = 2), " K, 1D ", round(peak(q1, s1), digits = 2),
        " K, centre − wall ", round(centre_minus_wall(s6), digits = 2), " K")
end
K_r = 0.2   Bi = 21.7  rings 357.57 K, 1D 358.99 K, centre − wall 48.07 K
K_r = 1.0   Bi = 4.3   rings 356.69 K, 1D 357.67 K, centre − wall 45.56 K
K_r = 10.0  Bi = 0.4   rings 356.0 K, 1D 356.1 K, centre − wall 16.3 K

The mean temperature of the 1D bed is within about 1.5 K of that on rings even where the centre and the wall differ by 45 K: the lumped coefficient gets the mean right, and the performance with it. The rings are for what the mean hides, such as the hottest sorbent at the centre, or the cooler and more loaded sorbent next to the wall.

Against an exact solution ​

Conduction across the rings can be checked against the exact solution for a cylinder that cools through its wall. A bed of argon, which the sorbent does not take up (CO₂ and N₂ are there in traces, for the isotherm), starts at = 320 K with no flow, inside a wall held at = 300 K by a very large heat capacity and wall coefficient. It then cools by conduction across its radius alone, and its temperature is the series

with the heat capacity of the bed per volume, almost all of it the sorbent's. The Bessel functions from their integral form, which the trapezoidal rule converges fast on, and the first zeros of by Newton's method:

julia
J(n, x; m = 400) = sum(cos(n * τ - x * sin(τ)) for τ in range(0, π, length = m + 1)[1:(end - 1)] .+ π / 2m) / m
β = map(1:8) do k
    x = (k - 0.25) * π
    for _ in 1:20
        x += J(0, x) / J(1, x)
    end
    x
end
θ(ρ, Fo) = sum(2 / (b * J(1, b)) * J(0, b * ρ) * exp(-b^2 * Fo) for b in β)
θ (generic function with 1 method)

The bed, 0.2 m long on 2 cells along it, as the column has no gradient along its length, on 8 rings, cooling for 600 s:

julia
K_r = 5.0
argon = Mocca.Gas(["CO2", "N2", "Ar"])
y_ar = [1e-9, 1e-9, 1 - 2e-9]
cooled = Mocca.Column(; length = 0.2, radius = 0.1445, outer_radius = 0.162, porosity = 0.37,
    radial_conductivity = K_r, wall_heat_transfer = 1e6, wall = Mocca.Wall(density = 1e6, heat_capacity = 1e4),
    outer_heat_transfer = 0.0)
fs_ar = Mocca.Flowsheet(argon, Mocca.Feed(argon; composition = y_ar, pressure = 1bar))
Mocca.add_bed!(fs_ar, :Bed, adsorbent, cooled; ncells = 2, radial_cells = 8, dispersion_velocity = 1.0)
Mocca.add_device!(fs_ar, :V_feed, :feed; at = (:Bed, :bottom))
Mocca.add_device!(fs_ar, :V_product, :product; at = (:Bed, :top))
conduction = Mocca.Process(fs_ar, Mocca.Cycle([Mocca.Idle(600.0)]);
    timestepping = Mocca.TimeStepping(max_dt = 2.0, first_dt = 2.0),
    initial_state = Dict(:Bed => (pressure = 1bar, temperature = 320.0, wall_temperature = 300.0, composition = y_ar)))
cooling = Mocca.simulate(conduction)

The Fourier number of each time follows from the heat capacity of the bed:

julia
ar_bed, ar_prm = conduction.flowsheet[:Bed], conduction.parameters[:Bed]
C = ar_prm[:SolidVolume][1] * ar_prm[:AdsorbentDensity][1] * ar_prm[:AdsorbentHeatCapacity][1] / ar_bed.data_domain[:volumes][1]
Fo(t) = K_r * t / (C * 0.1445^2)
Fo (generic function with 1 method)

The temperature of the rings (points) and the exact solution (lines), from the axis to the wall:

julia
r_rings = ar_bed.data_domain[:cell_centroids][2, 1:2:end]
ρ = range(0, 1, length = 101)
f_exact = Figure(size = (650, 420))
ax = Axis(f_exact[1, 1], xlabel = "r / r_in", ylabel = "T [K]")
t_cool = cumsum(cooling.timesteps)
for (k, t) in enumerate((60.0, 200.0, 600.0))
    color = Makie.wong_colors()[k]
    lines!(ax, ρ, 300 .+ 20 .* θ.(ρ, Fo(t)), color = color, label = "t = $(Int(t)) s, Fo = $(round(Fo(t), digits = 3))")
    s = cooling.states[findfirst(≈(t), t_cool)][:Bed]
    scatter!(ax, r_rings ./ 0.1445, s[:Temperature][1:2:end], color = color)
end
axislegend(ax, position = :lb)
f_exact

The rings follow the exact solution. The largest difference, as a share of the initial 20 K, is largest early on, when the wall has cooled only a thin layer next to it, and falls as the profile spreads across the bed:

julia
for t in (60.0, 200.0, 600.0)
    s = cooling.states[findfirst(≈(t), t_cool)][:Bed]
    e = maximum(abs.(s[:Temperature][1:2:end] .- (300 .+ 20 .* θ.(r_rings ./ 0.1445, Fo(t))))) / 20
    println("t = ", rpad(Int(t), 4), " s: ", round(100e, digits = 2), "%")
end
t = 60   s: 3.34%
t = 200  s: 1.15%
t = 600  s: 0.65%

Halving the width of the rings divides the error by about four (see Conduction across the radius): the rings are second order across the radius, including the half of the outer ring between its centre and the wall, through which the wall takes the heat.

A cycle on rings ​

A bed meshed across its radius works in any process. The four-step VSA of Haghpanah et al. (2013) on 20 × 4 cells, to its cyclic steady state, which cyclic_steady_state finds first for the same bed with one radial cell:

julia
vsa = Mocca.four_stage_vsa_process(ncells = 20, radial_cells = 4, column = column(0.2))
steady = Mocca.cyclic_steady_state(vsa)
Mocca.performance(steady)
(purity = 0.7927469997691787, recovery = 0.36152256615599576, specific_energy = 142.45065699236144, productivity = 0.23309235534588632, energy = 21740.647403764917, cycle_time = 100.0000000000003, feed = [2.6645184736109706, 15.098938017128834], extract = [0.9632835561498949, 0.25183748048647286], product = [1.7012349171628083, 14.847100536943595], adsorbent_volume = 0.0413262612032245, adsorbent_mass = 46.698675159643685)

The ports are the end rings, so the devices, steps and performance are as for any bed. write_results adds every cell of the bed in bed_fields.csv, and the summary the largest temperature difference across its radius over the cycle.
